# Selected Historical Pixel Style Guide

## Chosen Direction

The selected direction is **B. Folk Warm**. The production atlas should feel visibly Korean and hand-crafted while remaining practical for a small square-tile management game. It should carry the warmth of mineral pigment and folk-art simplification, but it must keep the clear silhouette discipline needed for 28px gameplay. When B's softness risks hurting readability, borrow C's stronger separation and winter clarity.

## Palette Rules

Spring uses fresh greens, damp soil browns, pale river blues, and light straw accents. Summer deepens the greens but should avoid turning every terrain tile into the same saturated field. Autumn shifts toward dry ochre grass, muted red-browns, darker timber, and harvest gold. Winter uses blue-gray snow, pale frozen river tones, dark exposed wood, and straw roofs with enough warm contrast that buildings do not disappear into snow.

Each season may change color temperature, but terrain identity should stay stable. Rivers keep a continuous blue or ice-blue band. Forests keep clustered tree masses. Mountains keep rocky, angular value contrast. Fertile ground keeps cultivated soil or crop texture. Hunting ground keeps brush, tracks, or scrub cues.

## Terrain Rules

Plain ground should be quiet and low-detail so buildings and residents remain readable. Forest tiles should use compact tree clusters with a recognizable canopy mass across all seasons. River tiles should be smooth and continuous, with frozen rivers still reading as the same path in winter. Mountain and rock tiles should be angular and darker than nearby ground. Fertile ground should show ordered cultivation, not generic grass. Hunting ground should use sparse brush, animal tracks, or broken vegetation without becoming noisy.

## Building Rules

Buildings should use late-Joseon frontier materials and simple functional silhouettes: timber frames, earth walls, straw thatch, dark roof accents, rough palisade logs, and beacon fire structures. The town center can be the most formal building, but it should still feel like a remote fortified settlement rather than a palace. Huts and ondol houses need distinct roof/body proportions. Storehouses should read as utilitarian storage. Palisades should be modular and repeatable. Beacons should read clearly even at small scale through mound, tower, fire bowl, or smoke-ready silhouette.

## Character Rules

Characters should be compact role silhouettes rather than detailed portraits. Settlers and workers use practical Joseon clothing shapes, muted fabric, and simple headwear. Watchmen and militia can have darker accents, polearms, banners, or guarded posture, but should not become fantasy soldiers. Northern raiders should contrast through rougher silhouettes, darker outerwear, and colder colors, without monster or villain caricature.

## Avoid List

- Generic medieval European fantasy houses, towers, castles, wizard props, or stone keeps.
- Modern tools, modern clothing, firearms, vehicles, signage, or UI text.
- Anime proportions, oversized heroic portraits, or highly rendered character art.
- Excessive folk ornament that makes tiles noisy at game scale.
- Single-season winter-only identity.
- Full-scene poster composition instead of separable production sprites.
